One original two-part micro-confession (180 characters):

> To: my older brother
> I was the one who broke Dad's guitar, not you. I let you take the blame for a year because saying sorry meant admitting I was the one who snuck out that night.

The admission is specific and honest — a named object (Dad's guitar), a named wrong (letting his brother carry the blame for a year). The reason it stayed in drafts is felt in the words, not stated: saying sorry would mean confessing a second secret, the night he snuck out, so the draft protects him twice. Swap the addressee and the confession breaks — only a brother could have taken that blame. It reads as typed and never sent, not written to be sent: no joke, no greeting, no lesson.
